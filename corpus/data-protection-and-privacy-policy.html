Data Protection and Privacy Policy

Document ID: ZDP-LEG-001
Effective date: 2024-01-30
Owner: Data Protection Officer (DPO), Legal & Compliance
Applies to: All Zidipay employees and any third party processing personal data on Zidipay's behalf

1. Purpose and scope

This policy explains how Zidipay processes personal data and the obligations every employee has in keeping that processing lawful. It is designed to satisfy the Kenya Data Protection Act, 2019 and the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) regulations, and to be consistent with GDPR-style principles applicable in the markets we serve.

2. Principles

Every member of Zidipay must process personal data in line with seven principles:

  1. Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency — there must be a lawful basis (consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public interest, or legitimate interests) for every processing activity.
  2. Purpose limitation — collect for a specified, explicit, and legitimate purpose; do not repurpose without a new lawful basis.
  3. Data minimisation — collect only what is necessary.
  4. Accuracy — keep data accurate and up to date; correct or erase inaccurate data without delay.
  5. Storage limitation — retain for no longer than necessary (see §6).
  6. Integrity and confidentiality — protect against unauthorised processing, accidental loss, destruction, or damage.
  7. Accountability — be able to demonstrate compliance with the above.

3. Lawful bases at Zidipay

Most processing at Zidipay relies on one of three lawful bases:

  • Performance of a contract — to provide the wallet, payment, or merchant service the customer has signed up for.
  • Legal obligation — KYC, AML monitoring, and reporting to regulators per the AML/KYC Policy.
  • Legitimate interests — fraud prevention, network security, and direct in-product communications related to a service the customer already uses. We document a legitimate-interest assessment (LIA) for each such processing.

4. Data subject rights

Data subjects (our customers, employees, and any other identifiable individuals whose data we hold) have the right to:

  1. Be informed — clear, plain-language notice of how we process their data, at the point of collection.
  2. Access — request a copy of the personal data we hold about them.
  3. Rectification — have inaccurate data corrected.
  4. Erasure — request deletion, subject to overriding legal obligations (for example, AML records).
  5. Restriction — ask for processing to pause while a dispute is being resolved.
  6. Data portability — receive their data in a structured, machine-readable format.
  7. Object — object to processing based on legitimate interests or for direct marketing.
  8. Not be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that has a legal or similarly significant effect, without safeguards.

Requests are submitted at privacy@zidipay.example. Zidipay responds within 30 calendar days, extendable by 30 days for complex requests with a written explanation. No fee is charged for the first request in any 12-month period.

5. Consent

Where consent is the lawful basis, it must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous, captured by an affirmative action (no pre-ticked boxes), and as easy to withdraw as to give. Consent records are stored against the customer record in the customer database.

6. Retention

Personal data is retained for the shortest period necessary:

  • Active customer account data — for the duration of the account.
  • Closed customer accounts — 7 years from closure, to satisfy AML and tax obligations.
  • KYC documents — 7 years from the end of the customer relationship.
  • Marketing consent and opt-out records — 3 years from the date of last contact.
  • Employee records — 7 years after the end of employment.
  • CCTV footage in offices — 30 days, except where retained for an incident.

At the end of the retention period, data is securely deleted or fully anonymised. Backups follow the same schedule, with a tolerance of up to 90 days for backup rotation.

7. International data transfers

Personal data may be transferred between the East-African countries Zidipay operates in to deliver the service. Transfers outside East Africa are made only where (a) the destination country provides adequate protection as determined by the ODPC, or (b) appropriate safeguards (standard contractual clauses) are in place, or (c) the data subject has explicitly consented after being informed of the risks. The DPO maintains the register of cross-border transfers.

8. Personal data breaches

A personal data breach is a security incident leading to accidental or unlawful destruction, loss, alteration, unauthorised disclosure of, or access to, personal data. Suspected breaches must be reported to security@zidipay.example or +254 711 000 999 within 60 minutes of discovery. The DPO, in coordination with the CISO, will determine whether to notify the regulator (the ODPC in Kenya and equivalents in UG/TZ/RW). Where required, the ODPC will be notified within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach. Affected individuals will be notified where the breach is likely to result in high risk to their rights and freedoms.

9. Vendors and processors

Any third party that processes personal data on Zidipay's behalf must sign a data processing agreement (DPA) and be on the approved vendor list. The vendor-onboarding checklist (Legal, Security, Procurement) includes a privacy and security review.

10. Training and accountability

Every Zidipay employee completes mandatory data protection training in their first 14 days and refresher training annually. Records of training completion are kept by People Operations.
